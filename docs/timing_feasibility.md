# Timing feasibility — stage 2

## Decision C: next-survey classification

**Timing estimates are unsupported for the specified cohort using the supplied local files. Retain next-survey classification and describe the interval as the next scheduled PATH wave, with variable elapsed time.** No duration model, fixed-year cumulative risk, first-relapse date, or survival curve is authorized. This is a decision about these local public-use files and this target, not a claim that every PATH release lacks timing information.

The files contain wave ordering, past-30-day and past-12-month smoking indicators, last-cigarette/quit recency, durations of selected quit attempts, and approximate end dates of last quit attempts. They do not provide a usable individual interview date/elapsed-interval field or a complete cigarette-specific resumption-event history for every person entering the proposed cohort. Those distinctions are why the proposal's time-to-relapse subtitle cannot be implemented honestly here.

## What was searched and what is available

`scripts/inspect_prediction_sources.py` converts all eight main codebooks and eight main adult questionnaires using `pdftotext -layout`, reads only the eight canonical TSV headers, indexes variable sections, and searches their titles plus questionnaire timing instructions. Search patterns and reviewed variable suffixes are saved in `configs/prediction_specification.json`. Results, file hashes, exact pages, ASK specifications, units and value labels are in `reports/prediction_source_evidence.json`. No raw survey rows were needed or inspected for this stage.

The header search covers INTVDATE, interview-date patterns, LASTINT, INTERVAL and ELAPSED, and the codebook-title search covers date, interview, relapse/resumption, last-smoked recency and quit-attempt durations. No usable individual interview-date or elapsed-interval field was found. Codebook section indexing covers all R-prefixed header variables and PERSONID; CASEID and early survey-design columns are outside that R-variable index and are identification/design fields, not interview clocks. The inventory also found no separate local linkage/timing file. Absence is scoped to this search and supplied files, not a proof about unavailable restricted data.

## Last cigarette versus cessation duration

Wave 1 AC1009 asks how long since completely quitting; waves 2–8 ask how long since the last cigarette. Unit labels are 1=Days, 2=Months and (except Wave 2) 3=Years. The existing derived DAYSQUIT variable names do not change their measurement meaning: these are converted recency reports, not a prospective event clock. The conversion algorithms use 30.4375 days per month and 365.25 per year, which are approximations, not precision about a quit date.

The raw items are asked under former/noncurrent use and prior-history routing; persistent former respondents often skip them. Wave 2 has a documented AC1009 skip/data error and permits only days/months. Wave 4 onward also separates current smokers' last-use recency (AC9029 and derived DAYSLASTUSED) from noncurrent-smoker AC1009/DAYSQUIT. That separation is why these fields cannot be swapped indiscriminately. Core-cohort entry does not require time-since-last-use data. The optional recent-quitter proxy is defined and qualified separately in the prediction specification.

## Retrospective questions: useful evidence, insufficient event history

**Some approximate event-related dates really are present.** AN0135 asks the approximate end date of the **last** quit attempt, paired with AN0135_VALUE_INFO where 1 means Date value. It is a character field in the codebook, not a guaranteed validated date for every respondent. Its eligibility depends on current tobacco use, a past-year quit attempt and an observed attempt duration. In early waves the displayed product can be tobacco products or a particular product depending on routing; later waves separate electronic and non-electronic tobacco. A former cigarette smoker using another product may be describing that product's quit attempt. Therefore the field is not automatically a cigarette relapse date, even for a valid-looking calendar value.

AN0130 asks how long the last attempt lasted; AN0145 asks the longest time abstinent within the past year under additional routing. Units include minutes, hours, days, weeks and months. These reports can describe unsuccessful attempts and multiple quit/resume cycles among selected people. AN0257 asks whether the time since last use is the longest abstinent spell in the past 12 months (1=Yes, 2=No), under recent-former-user and valid-time routing. None records every cigarette episode from the project's baseline, and absence from these routes is not event-free survival. An end date for the last unsuccessful quit attempt can follow an earlier post-baseline lapse, precede the chosen baseline, or concern another tobacco product. An abstinence duration cannot be assigned as time to relapse from an unrelated baseline.

These questions can distinguish reported use in the prior year from present abstinence, but they do not ask for every resumption since the immediately preceding interview. If the interview interval is longer than 12 months, early interval episodes can fall outside recall; if shorter than 12 months, recalled use can precede baseline. With no individual interval, that overlap cannot be resolved. Asking about smoking around this time 12 months ago is another status snapshot, not a record of every intervening event. No retrospective-question-derived “any interval resumption” endpoint is enabled.

## Missed events, observation and loss to follow-up

A baseline former smoker can resume briefly and quit again well before the next 30-day window; the primary endpoint then remains y=0. Repeated episodes, their order, their frequency, smoking intensity, the exact first cigarette and continuous abstinence between windows are not established. The follow-up window may capture brief experimentation or a person who has already stopped again; y=1 is not persistent regular smoking. Even two adjacent positive statuses would not prove uninterrupted smoking between interviews.

For classification, an observed valid y=0 is a negative for the specified snapshot, not an event-free survival censoring record. A missing/invalid follow-up gives no label; exclude that interval from supervised data and report attrition separately. Do not bridge a missing scheduled wave to a later interview or assume no event during the gap. Later observed adjacent pairs can be eligible afresh. Do not label missing individuals as dead or withdrawn based on absence alone. Death would be a competing event for an actual relapse analysis if verified, not sustained abstinence.

Nonresponse may be related to smoking and other risk factors, so complete-observation estimates can be biased. The local Wave 8 nonresponse report, Section 2.4 and preceding eligibility text, PDF p. 18, describes removal from further invitation after repeated nonresponse under specified rules. This is a concrete reason not to assume loss is random. The report's national weighting analyses do not make the local unweighted model nationally representative.

## Assessment of survival alternatives and assumptions

| Option | Assessment for this project |
|---|---|
| A — time to an actual resumption event | Rejected. Would require a documented common time origin, usable individual elapsed times, a cigarette-specific event tied to baseline, first-event identification, and correct observation/censoring rules. The supplied recency/last-attempt fields do not meet these conditions for the full cohort. |
| B — time to first observed smoking status | A descriptive **first positive wave index** could be defined in a different repeated-follow-up project, but it is a detection-order endpoint, not elapsed time or first actual relapse. Missing waves and variable observation gaps require explicit handling. Without individual elapsed intervals, no calendar-time model is enabled. Merely numbering waves does not supply duration information. |
| C — next-survey classification | Selected and configured. Requires only valid baseline status and valid status at the immediately next scheduled interview. The observation window and incomplete event coverage are stated plainly. |

If future data permit survival work, its protocol must be separately specified before fitting. Exact interview times would still only timestamp observation. A conventional first-relapse analysis would need to assess the assumption that unobserved episodes do not occur or are fully retrospectively captured; this is unsupported here and implausible given brief quit/resume cycles. Treating the first observed positive as an absorbing/persistent state would also require a persistence assumption not established by these status questions. Interval censoring actual first relapse requires knowledge that prior intervals were genuinely event-free, which a negative 30-day snapshot does not supply. Do not create artificial midpoint events.

Any censoring at last observed interview would need usable elapsed time and a justified conditional-independent-censoring assumption; attrition and repeated-nonresponse rules give reasons to question that assumption. Competing death and intermittent missing waves would require additional event/eligibility data. These assumptions are **not adopted** to make a survival model possible in this course project. A future first-observed-status model would have to retain that exact wording, account for detection gaps, and avoid claims about actual first smoking resumption or persistence.
