## Core task and prediction time

Among adult former established cigarette smokers who report no cigarette use in the past 30 days at a starting interview, predict whether they will report cigarette smoking in the past 30 days at the next scheduled PATH interview, conditional on that follow-up being observed with a valid derived outcome.

The unit of analysis will be a **person–adjacent-wave pair**. Prediction occurs at the end of the starting interview, after its answers are available and before any follow-up answers are known. A person may contribute several intervals and may re-enter after satisfying eligibility again; records are not independent participants. This is an observational, unweighted prediction study in the local adult files, not a national relapse-rate estimate.

`configs/prediction_specification.json` is the machine-readable contract. All eight waves are retained as sources and all seven adjacent scheduled pairs (1→2 through 7→8) are eligible. Wave 8 is a follow-up only because Wave 9 is not supplied. There is no requirement to appear in all eight waves. An absent Wave 2 is not replaced by Wave 3 when constructing 1→2. A returning participant may enter another genuinely adjacent observed pair later.

## Exact entry, outcome and exclusion rules

1. Start from the canonical adult file for wave t in `configs/project.json`. Adult instrument membership supplies the adult population definition; do not invent exact ages from age-category midpoints. The local Wave 8 nonresponse report, Table 2.1 footnote b, PDF p. 19, specifies 18+ for adult primary-wave interviews.
2. Keep a pair candidate only if the official starting-wave former-established indicator is exactly **1 (Yes)** AND its official derived past-30-day indicator is exactly **2 (No)**. “Former” alone is insufficient: someone who quit recently can still have smoked in the past 30 days. These valid derived answers may incorporate questionnaire routing/history; they are not necessarily literal answers to a single raw question.
3. Join the immediately following scheduled adult wave using PERSONID. Require a nonmissing join key and verify one record per PERSONID per wave before joining; duplicates are a later extraction-stage hard error, not a reason to silently take the first record. PERSONID is never a predictor. The PERSONID identification sections are indexed for every codebook in `reports/prediction_source_evidence.json`.
4. A valid following-wave past-30-day derived code **1** gives y=1; code **2** gives y=0. All other codes, system missing, empty strings or absent interviews have unknown outcomes. Do not impute labels or reinterpret skips as No. Exclude those pair records from supervised labeled data and report their counts separately from baseline exclusions.
5. No additional time-since-quitting availability, prior smoking transition, survival time, or future attendance requirement is imposed on the core cohort. Do not filter on future predictor completeness. No outcomes have been extracted, counted or split during this stage.

A negative label means only “no cigarette smoking in the follow-up past-30-day window,” not continuous abstinence since baseline. A positive label does not establish how often the person smoked, the first resumption date, or persistent relapse. The core estimand is conditional on observation; attrition can select the labeled cohort and limits generalization. Missing follow-up, death, institutionalization or withdrawal must never be coded as sustained abstinence. The adult TSVs alone do not establish each missing person's reason, so do not assign reasons without additional documentation/data.

## Cross-wave consistency and harmonization decision

The common cigarette-status construct is consistent enough for pooled **classification with explicit instrument differences**, not identical questioning or a constant observation interval. Use official derived indicators, rather than reconstructing status from a raw response that was legitimately skipped. Do not substitute unrevised names in waves 2–3.

- **Wave 1:** the former-established description says “more than 100,” but its actual algorithm requires `R01_AC1005=6`. That raw category is “100 or more cigarettes”; therefore the operational threshold is at least 100, consistent with later waves. The raw threshold and algorithm, not that imprecise prose, determine the interpretation. Former status requires `R01_AC1003=3` (Not at all). The derived past-30-day algorithm also uses recency and days smoked, so do not equate it blindly to the raw AC1004 question.
- **Wave 2:** select `R02R_A_FMR_ESTD_CIGS_REV`. Its threshold uses prior adult/youth information or current lifetime count, and noncurrent status is `R02_AC1003=3` OR `R02_AC1002_12M=2`. Recanters identified by `R02_AC1132=1` (“You have never smoked a cigarette”) are excluded by the official algorithm. Raw current-status and lifetime-count questions can be skipped because history or past-year answers already establish status.
- **Wave 3:** select `R03R_A_FMR_ESTD_CIGS_REV`. Lifetime threshold is `R03R_A_THRSHLD_CIGS=1`; noncurrent status is AC1003=3 or AC1002_12M=2, with recanting AC1010=3 excluded.
- **Wave 4:** lifetime threshold is the official historical/current `R04R_A_EVR_THRSH_CIGS`; the former rule uses AC1003=3 or AC1002_12M=2, excluding recanters. New and continuing/aged-up adults have different raw routing, handled by the supplied derived indicators.
- **Waves 5–8:** former algorithms additionally permit AC1002=2 in conjunction with the established-history threshold, while excluding recanting AC1010=3. The official historical ever/threshold construction matters; do not read AC1002 alone as a longitudinal never-smoker classification. Persistent former smokers can skip lifetime-count and last-use questions. Source status remains the official derived indicator.

These rules do not assert measurement invariance. Wave 1 lacks the later recanter-screening structure; replenishment, routing and interview mode also change. The configured **Wave 1 exclusion sensitivity** retains 2→3 through 7→8 to assess the baseline-instrument difference. Future reports must show results by interval alongside any pooled summary; differing populations and observation gaps can affect performance/prevalence. The number of later sensitivity analyses is fixed here; do not add favorable subsets after final-test inspection.

## Allowed inputs and leakage boundary

Only questionnaire/derived information observable by the end of interview t, or earlier, may be candidate predictors. Examples to review in the future feature dictionary are demographics, historical cigarette use, baseline last-use recency, baseline other-tobacco use, household environment and baseline health/behavior responses. This stage authorizes the temporal boundary, not an unverified list of survey features. Stage 3 now supplies 25 documented candidate names through `configs/feature_mapping.json` and `docs/feature_dictionary.csv`. The prediction contract links to that candidate allowlist; this is documentation-based eligibility, not a fitted or outcome-selected feature set. No training may run with an undocumented feature set.

PERSONID is join/group metadata only. Follow-up responses, future attendance, follow-up smoking/cessation fields, future summaries, label-derived features, and retrospectively constructed “time to relapse” are forbidden predictors. Current/earlier status used for eligibility is not a varying risk feature in the core abstinent cohort. Starting wave and the known planned next wave may be interval metadata and evaluation strata; they never imply known person-specific follow-up duration. Calendar fields and approximate quit-attempt dates are excluded from the app/core feature candidates here.

Before learning preprocessing, imputation, feature selection, resampling, tuning, probability calibration or thresholds, split participants with PERSONID so a person's records cannot enter both fitting and held-out participant groups. Keep natural evaluation prevalence. Reserved final-test outcomes remain untouched until the evaluation stage. No split or final-test record has been created or opened in stage 2; only dataset headers and documentation were inspected.

## Prespecified secondary analyses

**Recent last-cigarette recency subgroup.** Within the core cohort, use observed baseline `AC1009_NN` and `AC1009_UN` only. Compute approximate days as number × {1 day, 30.4375 days/month, 365.25 days/year}, accepting positive valid numbers and documented units. Select **30 < approximate days ≤ 365.25**. This is an observed recent-quitter proxy, not proof of continuous abstinence or a quit date. Wave 1 asks time since completely quitting; waves 2–8 ask time since last cigarette. This wording difference must accompany subgroup results. All core rules still apply, and the subgroup's primary label is unchanged.

Wave 2 permits only unit 1=Days and 2=Months; the other waves allow 3=Years. Do not import a nonexistent Wave 2 derived DAYSQUIT field, carry a previous recency forward, or add a general wave gap to it. Questionnaire skip rules often omit the question for persistent former smokers; missing is **unknown recency**, not zero days and not automatically long-term abstinence. The subgroup therefore describes respondents with observed recency and may have selection bias. Report subgroup coverage/missingness by wave before interpreting its performance. The conversion follows the documented derived algorithms (e.g., Wave 1 DAYSQUIT PDF p. 1208, Wave 3 p. 1789); it creates approximate history units only.

The Wave 2 questionnaire's AC1009 section documents incorrect skipping for some Wave 1 former/experimental former smokers answering AC1003=3, corrected during collection on **January 29, 2015**. It also flags missing-not-ascertained data. Preserve that missingness; do not reconstruct undocumented answers. As a rounding sensitivity, exclude raw reports of 1 month, 12 months or 1 year from this subgroup comparison: those lie near the 30-day/one-year cutoffs and do not warrant invented daily precision.

**Current established smoking at follow-up.** Within the primary labeled cohort, use following-wave `CUR_ESTD_CIGS`: 1=Yes, 2=No. Require its own valid code; report any additional exclusions and denominator. Its positive construct is an established lifetime history plus current every-day/some-day smoking. It can disagree with past-30-day smoking (e.g., intermittent current smoking with no recent use, or recent puffs followed by quitting). It is not persistent relapse; no future-wave persistence criterion is applied.

**Wave 1 exclusion sensitivity.** Use the same cohort/outcome rules for 2→3 through 7→8, without redefining outcome or thresholds. This assesses the initial-wave instrument difference rather than removing inconvenient outcomes.

**Observed cessation transition.** Defined but disabled: an optional future separate analysis could require observed smoking at t−1 followed by core abstinence at t. It is not an entry condition for the core task and has not been extracted or counted. No survival, persistent-relapse or “any interval resumption” endpoint is enabled.

## What the Streamlit app may claim

Before a model is trained and evaluated, the app may describe the task and demonstrate documented inputs; it may not display fabricated fitted predictions. After validation, the permitted output is **“Estimated probability of reporting cigarette smoking in the past 30 days at the next PATH survey, for people meeting the baseline eligibility criteria.”** It must identify the starting/next-wave context, state that follow-up intervals vary and individual elapsed intervals are unavailable locally, and disclose that validation is on observed follow-up respondents. A pooled probability reflects the study's mixture of intervals and respondents, not a universal future horizon for a present-day user.

Display a research-prototype label and the actual held-out validation/calibration evidence before calling estimates reliable or using risk categories. No low/medium/high category cutoffs are approved here. No personal confidence percentage, clinical prognosis, intervention recommendation, exact relapse date, time-to-relapse, persistent relapse, continuous abstinence, national representativeness, causal interpretation of SHAP, guaranteed performance, or one-/two-/three-year risk may be claimed. The demonstration must collect no PERSONID or raw records.

## Verification and stage boundary

Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/inspect_prediction_sources.py` to regenerate documentation evidence, then `.venv/bin/python scripts/validate_prediction_specification.py`, `.venv/bin/python scripts/check_prediction_evidence.py` (independent cited-page checks), and `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q`. Verify original hashes with `.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_inputs.py`. The evidence validator checks canonical header presence, revised-name mapping, documented Yes/No labels and questionnaire support; it does not replace scientific reading or establish ID uniqueness or cohort size. No participant-level extraction, training or evaluation is part of this stage. Next work requires a separately authorized prompt for detailed feature harmonization/data preparation.
